Euler's identity is \(e^{i\pi}+1=0\), which follows from the series expansion and is often called the most beautiful equation in mathematics.

The roots of a quadratic are given by $$\displaystyle x = \frac{-b \pm \sqrt{b^2-4ac}}{2a}$$ where the discriminant decides how many real roots exist.

For a right triangle, \(a^2 + b^2 = c^2\).
